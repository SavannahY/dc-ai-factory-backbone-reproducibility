# Nature Portfolio candidate references

These are candidate Nature / Nature Portfolio references that could strengthen
the manuscript without changing the existing numbered reference list yet.

## Highest-priority additions

1. Colangelo, P., Coskun, A. K., Megrue, J. et al. AI data centres as
   grid-interactive assets. Nature Energy 11, 254-261 (2026).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-025-01927-1

   Best use: add near the AI-load-dynamics paragraph and/or the discussion of
   dynamic buffering. This is the strongest Nature Portfolio support for the
   idea that AI data centres should not be treated only as passive loads. It
   reports a field demonstration on a 256-GPU cluster in a hyperscale facility,
   reducing power use during peak demand while maintaining AI quality-of-service
   constraints.

   Local copy:
   `downloads/ref24_colangelo_ai_data_centres_grid_interactive_assets_2026.pdf`.
   This is an author-provided Nature Energy PDF for local review; restrictions
   apply.

2. Kim, D., Dong, L. & Xie, L. Flexibility-aware framework for efficient
   planner-initiated siting of data center. Nature Communications (2026).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-72324-9

   Best use: add near the planning/interconnection discussion and the
   flexible-load framing. This is the strongest Nature Communications support
   for treating large AI/data-centre loads as controllable planning resources
   rather than fixed firm-load additions. It introduces firm, pause and shift
   flexibility envelopes for large-load siting, reports a 9-17% expansion of
   feasible locations at 1 GW and 19-21% at 2 GW in a synthetic Texas system,
   and argues that pre-certified flexibility envelopes can shorten first
   energization timelines.

   Local copy:
   `downloads/ref28_kim_flexibility_aware_data_center_siting_2026.pdf`.
   The Nature article access page is also saved as
   `downloads/ref28_kim_flexibility_aware_data_center_siting_2026_access_page.html`.

3. Xiao, T. et al. Environmental impact and net-zero pathways for sustainable
   artificial intelligence servers in the USA. Nature Sustainability 8,
   1541-1553 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41893-025-01681-y

   Best use: add in the introduction where the manuscript motivates
   geographically concentrated AI/data-centre load growth. This complements
   LBNL, EPRI and IEA by giving a Nature Portfolio analysis of U.S. AI-server
   energy, water and carbon impacts from 2024 to 2030.

   Local copy:
   `downloads/ref25_xiao_sustainable_ai_servers_usa_2025.pdf`.

4. Kemp, J. M., Millstein, D., Gorman, W. et al. Electric transmission value
   and its drivers in United States power markets. Nature Communications 16,
   8055 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-63143-5

   Best use: add in the public-planning / transmission-corridor paragraph. It
   supports the broader claim that transmission expansion and controllable
   transfer capacity are economically and operationally important in U.S. power
   systems. It also notes that DC lines offer greater power-flow control, while
   surrounding AC-system dynamics can still limit utilization.

   Local copy:
   `downloads/ref26_kemp_transmission_value_us_power_markets_2025.pdf`.

5. Yang, K., Wang, X., Chen, X. et al. Data-driven dynamic modeling for
   inverter-based resources using neural networks. Nature Communications 16,
   11696 (2025). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-66604-z

   Best use: add in the technical-risk paragraph about EMT validation and
   converter-dominated dynamics. It is not about data centres specifically, but
   it is a strong Nature Communications source for the claim that inverter-based
   resource dynamics are complex and require better dynamic models for reliable
   transient-stability assessment.

   Local copy:
   `downloads/ref27_yang_inverter_dynamic_modeling_2025.pdf`.

## Useful but lower priority

6. Alissa, H., Nick, T., Raniwala, A. et al. Using life cycle assessment to
   drive innovation for sustainable cool clouds. Nature 641, 331-338 (2025).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-08832-3

   Best use: only add if the manuscript keeps a broader sustainability or data
   centre infrastructure motivation. It is high-prestige and directly about
   cloud/data-centre infrastructure, but it focuses on cooling and life-cycle
   assessment rather than subtransmission power delivery.

7. Birahim, S. A. A net-grid-benefit test for interconnecting AI data centres.
   npj Environmental Social Sciences 1, 8 (2026).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s44432-026-00013-5

   Best use: possible discussion citation for the policy framing that AI data
   centres should be conditional grid participants rather than passive loads.
   It is a Comment/Perspective rather than primary technical evidence, so it
   should not carry a core technical claim.

8. Kassem, A., Sabra, H., Ali, A. A. et al. A settingless fault detection
   approach for MVDC network. Scientific Reports 16, 8267 (2026).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-38187-2

   Best use: optional support for the statement that MVDC protection and fast,
   selective fault detection remain active research problems. It is close to the
   manuscript's DC-protection risk paragraph, but CIGRE/IEEE sources should
   remain the primary standards/engineering support.

9. Xu, Y., Junaid, M., Li, M. et al. A novel controllable capacitor commutation
   based superconducting hybrid direct current breaker. Scientific Reports 14,
   10523 (2024). https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-61129-9

   Best use: optional support for DC-breaker feasibility and fault-current
   interruption research. Use only if the manuscript needs an additional open
   Nature Portfolio source in the DC-protection discussion.

## Recommended insertion strategy

Do not add all nine. For a compact Nature Communications submission, the best
set is probably refs. 1-5 above:

- Add Colangelo et al. near the AI training dynamics paragraph.
- Add Kim, Dong and Xie near the flexible-load planning/interconnection
  paragraph.
- Add Xiao et al. near the LBNL/EPRI/IEA data-centre growth sentence.
- Add Kemp et al. near the San Jose/transmission planning paragraph.
- Add Yang et al. near the technical-risk paragraph on EMT validation.

The Scientific Reports DC-protection papers are technically relevant but should
stay optional because the manuscript already cites CIGRE guidance and does not
need more speculative protection details unless reviewers ask.
